Participants:

Discussion:
- College funding: Carlos Ramirez and Sofia Ramirez want a plan for their daughter's college costs, which start in about three years. Victor Alvarez reviewed two approaches: earmark part of the joint taxable account and keep contributing to it, or open a dedicated 529 plan. The family values flexibility; Victor Alvarez will model both.
- Tax-exempt income: the Municipal Income Fund position in the joint account continues to provide federally tax-exempt income. With no state income tax in Texas, the federal exemption is the main benefit for the Ramirez Household. Carlos Ramirez asked whether to add more; Victor Alvarez said the current weight fits the household's Balanced profile.
- Rates: Sofia Ramirez asked what expected rate cuts mean for municipal bonds. Victor Alvarez explained that lower rates generally lift the value of bonds already held, while reinvested income and new purchases earn lower yields over time. The firm's fixed income team expects cuts to come more gradually than the market hopes, so he sees no reason to change the position ahead of them.
- Healthcare: Sofia Ramirez asked about recent GLP-1 drug news. Victor Alvarez explained that the household already has exposure through the Healthcare Innovation Fund, held in all three accounts, whose holdings include Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk. In the firm's view GLP-1 therapies are a multi-year theme; he cautioned against buying a single stock on headlines.
- Outcome: no portfolio changes.
