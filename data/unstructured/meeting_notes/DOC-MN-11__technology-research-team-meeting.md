Discussion:
- Arjun Mehta opened with his semiconductor supply chain checks. His read is that high-bandwidth memory (HBM) is effectively sold out through 2026 and that Micron is gaining share. He sees TSMC advanced packaging (CoWoS) capacity, rather than wafer supply, as the binding constraint on GPU supply next year; memory and packaging allocations now move together.
- Meredith Calloway: Nvidia remains the leader and nothing in the checks changes that view. She sees Broadcom's custom silicon programs for hyperscalers as a second growth vector that widens the opportunity beyond merchant GPUs. Custom silicon, and the networking that ties large clusters together, is the topic of Arjun Mehta's December report.
- Brendan Kessler is considering adding to Marvell and Arista Networks in the AI Infrastructure Fund, to lean into custom silicon and back-end networking. Both are existing positions; he wants sizing worked out before acting.
- Olivia Grant noted that the Global Technology Fund already holds about 8% in Nvidia, so clients who own both funds carry overlapping exposure, with smaller overlaps in names such as Broadcom and TSMC. She asked that the overlap be flagged whenever the team argues for a larger AI Infrastructure Fund weight.
- Meredith Calloway agreed the overlap belongs in the committee discussion. It does not change the research view on GPU supply or demand.
