Yesterday the Investment Committee approved IC-2026-005 by a 7-0 vote, shortening the Core Fixed Income Fund duration target from 6.0 to 5.0 years.

What it means for clients: nothing to trade. The change is implemented inside the fund - we are selling longer-dated Treasuries and reinvesting in shorter maturities over the coming weeks. Client allocations to the Core Fixed Income Fund do not change, and the Municipal Income Fund is unaffected.

Talking points:
- Why now: we think the market is pricing more rate cuts than are likely, and the path of policy rates is unusually uncertain. We expect Treasury yields to stay volatile and see a risk that the yield curve steepens.
- What it does: a shorter duration target reduces the fund's sensitivity to a rise in longer-term yields. The trade-off is less price upside if yields fall sharply.
- What it does not do: this is not a call that rates will spike, and it is not a move out of bonds. The fund remains an investment-grade core holding.
- Income: we expect the effect on the fund's yield to be modest.

The full analysis is in my March 2 note, "Rate Path and Duration Positioning", if clients want more detail. Send questions to me directly.
